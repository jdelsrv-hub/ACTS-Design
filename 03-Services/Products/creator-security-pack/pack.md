## Before you start

Getting hacked or doxxed is scary, and it happens to careful creators too. The good news: most attacks on creators use the same few tricks, and a few hours of setup blocks most of them.

This pack is the order we'd do it in. Each part has steps you can follow today and a short checklist. Part 7 is a one-page checklist to print and stick near your desk.

**How to use it**

- Do Parts 1 to 3 first. They stop most account takeovers.
- Work at a computer, with your phone next to you.
- Do one platform at a time. You don't have to finish in one sitting.
- Apps change their menus often. Where we give a menu path, we also link the official help page. If your screen looks different, follow the help page.

**Who it's for:** creators on YouTube, Twitch, Instagram and TikTok, including family-run channels. It's written in Australia, but nearly everything works anywhere. Australian help contacts are in Part 6.

**Honest limits:** this is general safety information, not legal or professional advice. If you've lost money, been threatened, or need a contract checked, talk to a professional: your bank, the police, a lawyer or IDCARE. Contacts are in Part 6.

### If you only do three things today

1. Turn on two-step login for your main email, using an authenticator app, a security key or a passkey (Part 1).
2. Print your backup codes and keep them somewhere safe at home (Part 1, step 5).
3. Never download a "contract", "media kit" or "game to review" from a brand you haven't checked (Part 3).

## Part 1: Lock down your accounts

**Time: about 60 to 90 minutes.**

### Why we start with your email

Your main email is the master key. Every platform sends password resets to it, so whoever controls that inbox can take over your YouTube, Instagram, TikTok and Twitch, one reset at a time. For most YouTube creators, it's also the Google Account that owns the channel.

So we lock down the email first, then work outwards. Do steps 1 to 8 on your email account, then repeat them on each platform using the guide at the end of this part.

### Step 1: Get a password manager

A password manager is an app that remembers all your passwords, creates strong new ones and fills them in for you. You only remember one password: the one that opens the manager.

**Our picks**

- **Free and great: Bitwarden.** The free plan covers unlimited passwords on unlimited devices. This is where we'd start on a budget.
- **Free, with extra privacy: Proton Pass** [affiliate: Proton Pass]. Ad: we earn a commission if you buy through these links, at no extra cost to you. The free plan covers unlimited passwords and devices, plus 10 "hide-my-email" aliases (more on those in Part 4). Paid plans add more aliases.
- **Your household mostly uses one brand of device?** The built-in Apple Passwords app or Google Password Manager is free and does the job.
- **Paid, polished and easy to share with family or a team: 1Password.** There's no free plan, so check the current price on their site.

**Set it up**

1. Install the manager on your phone and computer, and add its browser extension.
2. Make the main password a passphrase: four or more random words joined together, like `paddock-lantern-toast-orbit` (don't use this one). Long beats complicated.
3. Write the main password on paper and keep it somewhere safe at home. Most managers can't reset it for you if you forget it.
4. Turn on two-step login for the password manager itself (Step 4).
5. If it offers an emergency or recovery kit, print it and keep it with your backup codes.

### Step 2: Give every account its own password

Reused passwords are how most account takeovers start. When one website leaks its passwords, scammers try the same email and password everywhere else.

Let the password manager create a new password for each account, in this order:

1. Your main email, and any recovery email
2. YouTube (your Google Account), Instagram and Facebook, TikTok, Twitch
3. Your bank, PayPal, and anywhere you get paid (sponsor platforms, merch store, membership sites)
4. Everything else, a few at a time, as you log in

Many password managers can flag reused or leaked passwords. Fix the flagged ones first.

### Step 3: Use passkeys where you can

A passkey lets you sign in with your phone's fingerprint, face or PIN instead of typing a password. It only works on the real website, so a fake login page can't steal it. Google says passkeys give the strongest protection against phishing (being tricked into logging in on a fake site).

| Platform | Passkeys (as at 23 October 2026) | Where to add one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google / YouTube | Yes | [myaccount.google.com/signinoptions/passkeys](https://myaccount.google.com/signinoptions/passkeys) · [Google's guide](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/13548313) |
| Instagram | Yes, arriving with Meta's new "Meta Account" (rolling out since April 2026) | Accounts Center or Meta Account, then Password and security (may show as Login and security) |
| TikTok | Yes, on iPhone and Android | Settings and privacy, then look for Passkey (under Account or Security checkup) |
| Twitch | We couldn't confirm passkey support | Check [twitch.tv/settings/security](https://www.twitch.tv/settings/security) |

Keep your password and two-step login set up as well. They're your backup if you lose your phone.

### Step 4: Turn on two-step login (the right kind)

Two-step login (also called two-factor authentication or 2FA) means a stolen password isn't enough. A scammer also needs something only you have.

From strongest to weakest:

1. **Passkey** (Step 3).
2. **Security key.** A small USB or NFC key you plug in or tap. Best for the Google Account that owns your channel. Buy two and register both, so one is a spare. YubiKey is the best-known brand, and Google's Titan Security Key is another good option. Where to buy: [link: acTS web page for security keys]. Ad: we earn a commission if you buy through the links on that page, at no extra cost to you.
3. **Authenticator app.** An app on your phone that shows a new 6-digit code every 30 seconds. Free options: Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, 2FAS or Ente Auth (iPhone and Android), or Aegis (Android). Turn on the app's backup, or save your backup codes, so a lost phone doesn't lock you out.
4. **Text message (SMS) codes.** Better than nothing. But a scammer who takes over your mobile number (a "SIM swap") gets your codes too. Use SMS only where it's the only choice.

**Protect your mobile number.** Ask your phone company what extra protection they offer against your number being moved to another SIM, such as an account PIN or password, and turn it on.

### Step 5: Save your backup codes

Backup codes are one-time codes that get you in if you lose your phone or security key.

1. Find them in each platform's two-step login settings (links in the platform guide below).
2. Print them or write them down, and keep them somewhere safe at home. You can also keep a copy in your password manager.
3. Don't keep them in your email. If someone gets into your email, they'd get the codes too.
4. Each code works once. When you're running low, make a new set (the old set stops working).

### Step 6: Check your recovery email and phone

Recovery details are how a platform lets you back in. If they're old or wrong, you might not get back in, or a scammer might.

- Every account should have a recovery email and phone number that you still use.
- The recovery email should be one only you control, with its own two-step login. Not a shared family inbox, an old work email or a school email.
- On a family channel, the adult who owns the channel owns the recovery details.

### Step 7: Check where you're signed in

Every platform lists the phones, computers and browsers signed in to your account. Sign out of anything you don't recognise, phones you've sold, and computers you don't use any more. If something looks unfamiliar, change your password straight after.

### Step 8: Remove connected apps you don't use

Over time you connect stream tools, thumbnail sites, analytics apps and giveaway tools to your accounts. Each one is a door into your account. Remove anything you don't use or don't recognise. Keep the tools you use, from companies you trust.

## Part 2: Give your team access without sharing passwords

**Time: about 15 minutes per platform.**

Editors, moderators, managers and family members who help should each get their own access, not your password. That way:

- if a helper gets phished, your account isn't handed over with it
- you can see who did what
- when someone moves on, you remove them in one click, with no passwords to change.

**Four rules**

1. Give the lowest level of access that lets them do the job.
2. Everyone uses their own account, with their own two-step login turned on. Ask them.
3. Review your team list every three months, and remove people the day they stop helping.
4. Never share your stream key.

### YouTube: channel permissions

1. Go to [studio.youtube.com](https://studio.youtube.com) and click **Settings**.
2. Click **Permissions**, then **Invite**.
3. Enter their email address, choose a role under **Access**, and click **Done**.

Invites expire after 30 days. If your channel uses an older Brand Account, YouTube Studio will ask you to move to channel permissions first. [YouTube's guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9481328)

| Role | Good for | What to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner | You | Can do everything, including delete the channel. Keep this to yourself. |
| Manager | One business partner you'd trust with your house keys | Can do almost everything, including adding and removing people. Can't delete the channel. |
| Editor | Your editor or channel assistant | Uploads, edits and publishes. Can't manage permissions, delete published content, or delete or reset stream keys. |
| Editor (limited) | Most helpers | Same as Editor, but can't see revenue. |
| Subtitle editor | Captioners and translators | Subtitles only. |
| Viewer / Viewer (limited) | An accountant or manager who only needs to look | Can see channel data, but can't change anything. The limited version hides revenue. Viewers can see stream settings, but not your stream key. |

Our pick for most helpers: **Editor (limited)**.

### Twitch: roles

Go to your Creator Dashboard, then **Community**, then **Roles Manager**. Search for the person and tick the role. [Twitch's guide](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/Managing-Roles-for-your-Channel)

- **Moderator:** looks after chat: timeouts, bans and chat modes.
- **Lead Moderator:** everything a moderator does, plus managing the mod team and VIPs.
- **Editor:** helps run your channel from the dashboard, such as stream info, ad breaks and clips.
- **VIP:** a thank-you for trusted regulars. It's not a security role.

Only you, the channel owner, can add Editors and Lead Moderators. Keep Editor to one or two people, and choose mods who've been around a while and have two-step login on.

### Instagram: Meta Business Suite (free)

If your Instagram is a creator or business account, you can give helpers their own access through Meta Business Suite.

1. In Meta Business Suite, open **Settings**, then **People**, and add the person by email.
2. Give them **Partial access**, not Full control.
3. Assign your Instagram account and switch on only the tasks they need, such as content, messages, community activity or insights.

[Meta's guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2169003770027706)

**Paid option:** Instagram's **Shared access** lets people post, like and comment on your account without your password, with separate switches for your inbox and stories. It's part of Meta's paid Meta One plans and isn't available to teens. Prices vary by country, so check in the app (profile, menu, **Shared access**). Try the free Business Suite route first. [Meta's guide](https://help.instagram.com/514043488379093)

### TikTok: what's on offer

We couldn't find a free, simple team-roles feature for everyday TikTok creator accounts, like YouTube and Twitch have. Here are your options.

1. **TikTok Business Center** (free to set up). An admin links your account with a QR code you scan in the TikTok app, then gives team members access. With "Manage account" access, they can reply to comments, send messages and update your profile, and you choose whether new videos post to your profile. The catch: it only works with a Business Account, and Business Accounts can only use TikTok's Commercial Music Library. That may matter for your content. [TikTok's guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-managing-tiktok-accounts-in-business-center)
2. **A scheduling tool that connects to TikTok properly.** Many schedulers let a helper prepare and schedule posts without your password. Only use one that connects through TikTok's own login screen, never one that asks you to type your password into its site.
3. **Otherwise, keep the login to yourself.** Helpers can send you edits to post, or sit with you while you post.

### Part 2 checklist

- [ ] No one else knows your passwords
- [ ] YouTube helpers invited with the lowest role that works
- [ ] Twitch Editor and Lead Moderator given only to people you trust most
- [ ] Instagram helpers added through Meta Business Suite with Partial access
- [ ] TikTok: no shared password; team access only through Business Center or an official tool
- [ ] Everyone on your team has two-step login on
- [ ] Team review booked in your calendar every three months

## Part 3: Spot the scams aimed at creators

**Time: 20 minutes to read. After that, it's a habit.**

Scams aimed at creators are convincing. They use real brand names, real logos and real-looking deadlines. You don't need to spot every trick. You need three habits:

1. **Slow down.** Urgency ("24 hours or your channel is deleted") is the biggest red flag of all.
2. **Check through a second route.** Don't use the link, button or phone number in the message. Open the app yourself, or type the website address yourself.
3. **Never share codes.** No real platform, brand or support team will ask for your password, two-step code or backup codes.

### Scam 1: The fake sponsorship with a malware "contract"

**What it looks like:** an email from a brand offering a paid deal. It's often a big name, a game studio, a VPN, an AI tool or software company. To see the terms, you download a "contract", "brief" or "media kit", often a ZIP file with a password in the email. Or they ask you to install their game or app to review it.

**What actually happens:** the file installs malware called an info-stealer. It copies the saved passwords and "stay signed in" data from your browser. With that, scammers can get into your channel without your password or two-step code. Hijacked channels are often renamed and used for crypto-scam livestreams.

**Red flags**

- The "contract" is a file to download, not a document you can read in your browser
- A ZIP or RAR file, a password-protected file, or a file ending in `.exe`, `.scr`, `.bat`, `.lnk` or `.msi`
- The sender's address doesn't match the brand's real website, or comes from Gmail or Outlook
- A generous offer, but no questions about your audience
- Pressure to sign today
- A request to "sign in with Google" (or another platform) to see the offer
- A request to turn off your antivirus

**What to do**

1. Ask them to put the terms in the email, or send a document you can open in your browser.
2. Find the brand's official website yourself, contact them through it, and ask if this person works there.
3. Never run a program from a sponsorship email. Real contracts are documents (often a PDF or an e-signing service like DocuSign), never programs.
4. In Chrome, turn on Enhanced Safe Browsing: **Settings**, then **Privacy and security**, then **Security**, then **Enhanced protection**. It checks downloads more closely, and YouTube recommends it for creators.
5. Already opened something? Stop and go to Part 6 now.
6. For real deals: have a lawyer look over any contract with big money, long terms or exclusivity. This pack doesn't cover contract terms.

### Scam 2: Fake copyright strikes, policy warnings and verification offers

**What it looks like:** an email or DM saying you've got a copyright strike, broken a rule, or your account will be deleted unless you "appeal" or "verify" within 24 to 48 hours. Or an offer of a verified badge. Some arrive as a private video from "YouTube" announcing a change to monetisation rules.

**Good to know**

- YouTube says it will never contact you through a private video. If a "YouTube" video is privately shared with you, it's a scam.
- Real copyright claims and strikes show up in YouTube Studio.
- Instagram doesn't contact you about your account by DM. You can check whether an email really came from Instagram under **Recent emails** (Part 1).
- Scam emails can fake the sender's address, so a real-looking "from" line isn't proof.

**Red flags:** a short deadline, a link to a login page, an "appeal form" that wants your password or codes, a DM from an account called something like "Meta Support" or "Copyright Team", or a fee to get verified.

**What to do:** don't click. Open the app, or type the site address yourself, and check your notifications and account status. If nothing's there, it's fake. Report it, then delete it. You can forward fake Instagram emails to phish@instagram.com.

### Scam 3: Brand-deal impersonation

**What it looks like:** someone posing as a real brand, agency or "creator platform".

- An email address or website that's almost right: an extra letter, a hyphen, or `.co` instead of `.com`
- A collab platform that asks you to log in with your Google, Instagram or TikTok account on its own site, then steals your password and code as you type them
- "Ambassador" deals where you pay first: for products, shipping or a starter kit
- Fake agencies asking for an upfront fee, or your bank login to "set up payments"

**Red flags:** you pay before they pay; a login page that isn't on the platform's own website; payment by gift card or crypto; they won't do a video call; the contract comes from a personal email.

**What to do**

- Check the brand's official website and social accounts, and contact them through those.
- Ask for a short video call. Real brand managers will usually say yes.
- Never pay to be sponsored.
- Only log in on the platform's real website or app. Passkeys help here, because they won't work on a fake site.
- Report it to Scamwatch (Part 6).

### Scam 4: Account-recovery scammers

**What it looks like:** after a hack, or after you post about one, "recovery experts" and "ethical hackers" turn up in your comments and DMs. Some pretend to be platform staff.

**Good to know:** recovery is free, through the official links in Part 6. Google says it doesn't work with any service that claims to provide account or password support. Anyone who charges to "hack it back" is a scammer. Many take the money and disappear, or take more of your accounts.

**Red flags:** they found you first; they want money or crypto; they want your codes or remote access to your computer; they claim to work at Meta, TikTok, Google or Twitch.

**What to do:** ignore and block. Use only the official links in Part 6. If you've already paid, call your bank straight away, then report it to Scamwatch and ReportCyber.

### Scam 5: Giveaway and collab scams

**What it looks like**

- **Fake giveaways using your name:** copycat accounts (your name with an extra dot or underscore) comment on your posts or DM your followers: "You won. Claim here" or "Pay a small shipping fee".
- **Fake collabs:** "Let's collab. Test our new game first" (the download is malware), or "We'll feature you" for a fee.
- **Fake growth services:** views or followers for money, sometimes asking for your login.

**What to do**

- Pin a post explaining how your giveaways work, for example: "We only announce winners from this account. We'll never ask you for money, card details or a login."
- Report copycat accounts for impersonation (links in Part 6).
- Turn on comment filters for words like "winner", "claim", "DM me" and versions of your handle. Look for YouTube Studio's blocked words, Instagram's Hidden Words, TikTok's comment filters and Twitch's AutoMod and blocked terms.
- Never download a game or app from a collab request. Real game studios usually send a key for an official store like Steam.

### Where to report scams

- **The platform:** report the message or account in the app.
- **Australia:** [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam), even if you didn't lose money. If you lost money, call your bank first, then report to ReportCyber (Part 6).

### Part 3 checklist

- [ ] Never download or run "contracts", media kits or games from unchecked brands
- [ ] Check every deal through the brand's official website
- [ ] Ignore strike, deadline and "verify now" messages until you've checked in the app
- [ ] Never pay to get a sponsorship
- [ ] Never share passwords, two-step codes or backup codes
- [ ] Chrome Enhanced Safe Browsing on
- [ ] Pinned "how our giveaways work" post
- [ ] Comment filters on for scam words and copycat handles

## Part 4: Stop doxxing

**Time: about 60 minutes, then a few habits.**

Doxxing is when someone finds and shares your personal details, like your home address, to scare or harm you. Most creators who get doxxed never posted their address. Small clues added up.

### Step 1: Search yourself first

Open a private browser window and search like a stranger would:

- your real name with your suburb
- your creator name with "real name" or "address"
- your phone numbers and email addresses
- your street address.

Note what you find. Steps 5 to 7 help you clean it up.

### Step 2: Check what your content gives away

Go through your last 20 posts and your next few videos with this list.

| Clue | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Location data in photos and videos.** Phones can save your GPS location inside every photo and video. | Turn it off at the camera (Step 3). Some platforms strip it on upload, but you can't count on it, and files you send directly (email, cloud links, your website) often keep it. |
| **Window views.** Landmarks, distinctive buildings, hills, street signs, house numbers, the neighbour's car. | Film facing away from windows, close the blinds, or blur it. |
| **Parcels and mail.** Address labels in unboxings, letters on the desk, magazine labels. | Peel off labels before filming. Get deliveries to a PO Box or locker (Step 5). |
| **Reflections.** Glasses, mirrors, windows, TV and monitor screens, shiny appliances, even eyes in close-ups. | Zoom in on your shot and thumbnail before you post. |
| **School uniforms and logos.** Uniforms, hats, sports club shirts, name tags. | Film before uniforms go on or after they come off. Cover or blur logos. |
| **Your car.** Number plates, dealership stickers, parking permits. | Blur plates and stickers. |
| **Outside your home.** Front door, letterbox, house number, the view from the driveway. | Don't film outside your home. |
| **Live location.** Streaming outdoors, location tags, location sharing in apps, fitness app routes that start and end at home. | Post after you've left. Turn off location tags. Share live location only with people you know offline. Set fitness apps to hide the start and end of your routes. |
| **Routines.** The same café every Saturday, school pick-up times, "We're away for two weeks." | Post later, not live. Share holidays after you get home. |
| **Old content.** Early posts from before you were careful. | Review your first year of posts and archive anything risky. |

### Step 3: Strip location from photos and videos

**iPhone**

- **Stop saving it:** **Settings**, then **Privacy & Security**, then **Location Services**, then **Camera**, then **Never**.
- **Share without it:** in Photos, tap **Share**, then **Options**, and turn off **Location**. You'll see "Location Not Included".
- **Remove it from a photo you've taken:** see [Apple's guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/manage-location-metadata-in-photos-ips0d7a5df82/web).

**Android** (menu names vary by phone)

- **Stop saving it:** **Settings**, then **Location**, then **App location permissions**, then your camera app, then **Don't allow**. Or turn off location in your camera app's own settings.
- **Google Photos:** location sharing is set for each shared album or link. Check it's off. Photos you download and send elsewhere keep their original location. [Google's guide](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6153599)

**Computer**

- **Windows:** right-click the photo, choose **Properties**, then **Details**, then **Remove Properties and Personal Information**.
- **Mac:** open the photo in Preview, press Command-I, open the GPS tab and choose **Remove Location Info**, then save.

### Step 4: Use a separate business email and phone number

- **Business email:** make a new email only for brand deals, your channel's About section and your website. Keep your personal email private.
- **Important:** your public business email should not be the email that logs in to your channels. Keep that one private.
- **Business phone:** get a second number with a prepaid SIM or eSIM (many phones take two). Use it anywhere public. Keep your personal number off your channels, website and media kit.
- **Email aliases:** an alias is a stand-in email address that forwards to your real inbox. Use a different one for each tool, giveaway or newsletter you sign up for, and switch it off if it starts getting spam. Your real address stays private. ### Step 5: Get a PO Box or Parcel Locker

- **Australia Post Parcel Lockers** are free to use with a free MyPost account. [Australia Post](https://auspost.com.au/personal/receiving/parcel-deliveries/parcel-lockers)
- **A PO Box** has a yearly fee and gives you an address for your website, invoices and brand paperwork. [Australia Post PO Boxes](https://auspost.com.au/business/receiving/po-boxes-and-locked-bags)
- Some couriers can't deliver to PO Boxes or lockers. For those, ask brands to send to a Post Office collection point.

**Registered a business name in Australia?**

- ASIC's public business names register always shows your "address for service of documents". Use a PO Box or your accountant's address, not your home.
- If your main place of business is your home, the register shows only your suburb, state and postcode.
- If you're worried about your safety, you can ask ASIC to hide some personal details. [ASIC's guide](https://www.asic.gov.au/online-services/online-services-information-and-support/personal-information-shown-on-the-business-names-and-companies-registers)
- Check with your accountant before changing business addresses.

### Step 6: Turn on domain privacy (if you own a website)

When you register a domain, your contact details go into a public directory called WHOIS.

- **.com, .net and most others:** turn on WHOIS privacy (sometimes called "redaction") in your domain settings. Many registrars include it free. Check yours at [lookup.icann.org](https://lookup.icann.org).
- **.au domains (.com.au, .au):** there's no privacy service, but the public listing shows only the registrant's name and a contact email, not your address or phone. Use your business email as the contact. Check yours at [whois.auda.org.au](https://whois.auda.org.au).

### Step 7: Clean up search results and people-search sites

- **Google "Results about you":** ask Google to remove search results that show your phone number, home address or email, and get alerts when new ones appear. It's available in Australia. Search for "Results about you" while signed in to Google. It removes the result from Google Search, not the page itself, so contact the website too. [Google Australia's announcement](https://blog.google/intl/en-au/products/explore-get-answers/results-about-you/)
- **Google Street View:** you can ask Google to blur your house. Open the Street View image, click **Report a problem**, and choose your home. The blur is permanent. [Google's guide](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/15439776)
- **People-search and data-broker sites:** look for an "opt out" or "remove my information" link, usually at the bottom of the page. Paid removal services exist, but most are built for US sites, so check they cover your country before paying.
- **Electoral roll (Australia):** the public roll shows your address. If publishing it could put you or your family at risk, you can apply to the AEC to be a "silent elector". You'll need a statutory declaration explaining the risk. [AEC: silent electors](https://www.aec.gov.au/Enrolling_to_vote/Special_Category/silent-electors.htm)
- **Old accounts:** delete or lock down old social profiles, forums and blogs you don't use.

### Step 8: Keep kids and family safe on camera

**Account rules first.** In Australia, under-16s can't hold their own accounts on age-restricted platforms, including YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and Twitch (since 10 December 2025). On a family channel, an adult owns the account, the email and the recovery details. [eSafety: which platforms are age-restricted](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions/which-platforms-are-age-restricted)

**On camera**

- Use nicknames, not full names. Leave out full birth dates.
- Keep school names, uniforms, logos, teachers' names and sports club details out of shot.
- Post school events, park trips and holidays after you've left, not while you're there.
- Check every background for school notes on the fridge, calendars, name labels and artwork with names on it.
- Keep bath time, getting changed and swimwear moments off camera.
- Ask older kids before you post them, and respect a no.
- Agree a family rule on who can film and who can post.

eSafety has more help for parents at [esafety.gov.au/parents](https://www.esafety.gov.au/parents).

### If you've been doxxed

1. **If you're in danger, call 000.**
2. **Save evidence before anything else:** screenshots, links, usernames, dates and times.
3. **Report the post or account** to the platform.
4. **Tell the people you live with**, and lock down your accounts (Part 1).
5. **Report to the police.** In Australia, sharing someone's personal details in a menacing or harassing way is a federal crime. For non-urgent help, call the Police Assistance Line on 131 444.
6. **Serious abuse:** if the platform doesn't act, eSafety may be able to help (Part 6).
7. **Look after yourself.** It's a lot to deal with. Talk to someone you trust, or call Lifeline on 13 11 14.

## Part 5: Stream safety

**Time: 30 minutes to set up, then 5 minutes before each stream.**

Most stream leaks are accidents: a notification, an email tab, an address that autofills on screen. Set things up once, then do a quick check before every stream.

### Set up once

1. **Check every OBS scene and source.** Click through each scene, and each source in it. Delete old sources you don't use. A forgotten browser or window source can show up again later.
2. **Capture windows, not your whole screen.** Use Window Capture or Game Capture instead of Display Capture. Display Capture shows everything on that screen, including pop-ups, emails and messages.
3. **Make a privacy scene.** A "Be right back" screen you can switch to instantly. Set a hotkey for it in OBS under **Settings**, then **Hotkeys**.
4. **Turn on Studio Mode in OBS.** It lets you preview a scene before it goes live.
5. **Make a streaming-only browser profile,** with no saved passwords, addresses or cards, no bookmarks bar and no personal history. In Chrome, click your profile picture and add a new profile. Then, in that profile, turn off address and payment autofill under **Settings**, then **Autofill and passwords**. Autofill can pop your home address onto the screen the moment you click a form.
6. **Silence notifications while live.** On Windows 11, go to **Settings**, then **System**, then **Notifications**, then **Do not disturb**. You can set it to turn on automatically when you're playing a game or using an app full screen. On a Mac, use Focus. Put your phone on Do Not Disturb too.
7. **Discord Streamer Mode.** In Discord's **User Settings**, turn on Streamer Mode. It can switch on automatically when OBS is running, hides personal details like your email, and mutes notifications.
8. **Add a stream delay.** In OBS, go to **Settings**, then **Advanced**, then **Stream Delay**. With a delay on, if something private slips onto the screen, choose **Stop Streaming (discard delay)** and viewers never see it. A delay also stops people tracking you in real time. Twitch Partners can also set a delay in Stream Manager. The trade-off: chat sees everything a little later.
9. **Protect your stream key.** It lets anyone stream to your channel. Never show your stream settings on screen, never share the key, and reset it if it's ever shown.

### Before every stream

- [ ] Close emails, messages and any tabs you don't want seen
- [ ] Open only your streaming browser profile
- [ ] Do Not Disturb on (computer and phone)
- [ ] Click through your scenes in Studio Mode
- [ ] Look behind you on camera: mail, calendars, whiteboards, photos, windows, reflections
- [ ] Phone screen out of shot
- [ ] Privacy scene hotkey ready
- [ ] Screen-sharing in a call or collab? Share one window, not the whole screen

### Outdoor and IRL streams

- Start and end streams away from home. Never stream your arrival home.
- Use a longer delay (minutes, not seconds) so no one can find you in real time.
- Keep street signs, house numbers and your number plate out of shot.

## Part 6: If you get hacked

Take a breath. This is fixable, and acting fast makes recovery much more likely. Work through this list in order.

### In the first hour

1. **Opened a file or installed something? Use a different device.** If the hack started with a download, the scammer may still be on that computer. Do the steps below from your phone or another device you trust. Don't log in on the affected computer again until it's been scanned with antivirus software, or checked or reset by a computer repair professional.
2. **Secure your email first.** Change the password, sign out of all other devices, check the recovery email and phone, and look for forwarding rules or filters you didn't make (Part 1, Gmail extra check).
3. **Recover the account** using the official links below. Only use these links, typed yourself. Platforms never charge for recovery and never DM you first.
4. **Sign out everywhere,** as well as changing your password. If an info-stealer copied your "stay signed in" data, a new password alone may not kick the scammer out.
5. **Remove what the scammer added:** unknown devices, connected apps, new team members, new emails or phone numbers, and changed payout details. On YouTube, use the [channel cleanup tool](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14849770) in YouTube Studio once you're back in. Reset your stream keys.
6. **Change passwords** on any account that shared that password, and on every account that was saved in the affected computer's browser.

### Later today

7. **Money:** if card or bank details might be exposed, call your bank now. Check PayPal and your platform payout settings.
8. **Tell people:** post on your other accounts that you've been hacked, and ask followers not to click links or send money. Let brand partners and your team know.
9. **Write it all down:** screenshots, dates, times, emails you received and usernames. Support teams and police will ask.
10. **Report it** (Australian help below).

## Part 7: Your one-page checklist

Print this page and tick it off. Redo it every six months, and any time you change phones.

**Lock down your accounts**

- [ ] Main email secured first: unique password and two-step login
- [ ] Password manager set up; main password on paper at home
- [ ] Every account has its own password
- [ ] Passkeys added where offered
- [ ] Two-step login on everywhere, using an app, key or passkey over SMS
- [ ] Extra protection on your mobile number
- [ ] Backup codes printed and stored safely, not in your email
- [ ] Recovery email and phone up to date
- [ ] Unknown devices signed out; unused apps removed

**Your team**

- [ ] No shared passwords
- [ ] Helpers on their own access, lowest role that works
- [ ] Team list reviewed every three months

**Scams**

- [ ] Never download "contracts", media kits or games from unchecked brands
- [ ] Check deals through the brand's real website
- [ ] Strike or deadline message? Check in the app, never the link
- [ ] Never pay to be sponsored; never share codes

**Doxxing**

- [ ] Camera location off on every phone in the house
- [ ] Check windows, parcels, reflections and uniforms before posting
- [ ] Business email and number; PO Box or Parcel Locker
- [ ] Domain privacy on
- [ ] Post locations after you've left

**Streaming**

- [ ] Window Capture, not Display Capture
- [ ] Privacy scene with a hotkey; stream delay on
- [ ] Streaming-only browser profile, no autofill
- [ ] Do Not Disturb on; stream key never on screen

**If you're hacked**

- [ ] Clean device, then email first, then official recovery links
- [ ] Sign out everywhere; remove what the scammer added
- [ ] Bank, followers, brand partners told; reports made
